## Four Beats

1. **Name the job.** Give the Carlson Survey survey job a short title before you lose the traverse.
2. **Pin the live points.** Keep current shots on Carlson Survey field data and leave unused traverses aside.
3. **Read the COGO.** Open Carlson Survey COGO tools before you move a point that already has a plat.
4. **Check the plat.** Read Carlson Survey plat drafting before you archive a job.
5. **Keep the pair.** Leave field data and COGO on the same Carlson Survey survey job.
6. **Leave the plat ready.** Keep drafting aligned with Carlson Survey field data.
7. **Read the traverse again.** Open Carlson Survey COGO tools if the plat still looks crowded.
8. **Close the loop.** Clear finished jobs so the next Carlson Survey visit does not start with a search.

## First Questions

**Does Carlson Survey replace every other program?**
No. Carlson Survey sits beside the field list, browser, and files you already use.

**Can two people share one computer?**
Yes. Each person keeps a separate Carlson Survey profile.

**What should a new user try first?**
One small Carlson Survey field set today, then the same plat drafting tomorrow.

**Does plat drafting leave the survey job?**
No. Carlson Survey plat drafting stays with Carlson Survey COGO tools on that job.

**Is Carlson Survey only for a finished plat?**
No. Carlson Survey can hold a single traverse while the rest of the site stays off the board.

**Does the plat need a second CAD file?**
No. Carlson Survey keeps field data and plat drafting on this survey job.

**Will the next person still see the traverse?**
Yes. A named Carlson Survey survey job keeps the points attached to the plat.

## Field Note

Carlson Survey is easier to trust when the survey job stays smaller than the folder it came from. A named job shows what is active, the point pins show what you will draft today, and the closing note tells the next person which traverse changed.

The same loop fits a single traverse, a short plat, or a desk shared by two people. Open the last Carlson Survey survey job, finish one plat, and leave fewer lost-traverse files than you found.

A review should not have to guess which points belong to the open plat. Keep that pair together inside Carlson Survey.

Other programs stay where they are. Carlson Survey only holds the field data, the COGO tools, and the handoff.

The record is short on purpose: not a copy of every shot, only the path back to the plat that is still open.

## One Real Pass

Pick a job that already has a name. Open it in Carlson Survey, keep only those points on the field data, and finish one plat you can see.

If the pass feels crowded, the set is too wide. Drop anything that belongs to a different site and leave it out of this Carlson Survey survey job.

A pass is done when that plat is readable and the extra files are no longer in the way.

The next pass can have its own title. What matters is that this survey job stays readable from the first point to the last, with the plat still matching the traverse.
